Services

What a person at CyberWall actually does

The work is updates to known security holes, tested before they touch the live shop, then explained in plain English. Tools help us watch and check. People decide what matters and what the note to you says.

What is included

Which checks you get depends on the plan. The tags match the proposed prices. Nothing below is a promise that a store cannot be hacked.

Watching for holes that affect your store

All plans

The team follows security news for the software your store uses. The aim is to notice relevant items early, including news that is not on the biggest public lists yet. That is a goal for how the work is done. It is not a promise that we will always be first.

A private copy before the live store

All plans

Each fix is tried on a private copy first. Technicians call that copy staging. If the fix fails, your customers never see it.

A checkout test

All plans

We check that a customer can still add to cart and pay. A locked-down shop that cannot take an order has not been looked after.

A plain-English note

All plans

After a fix, a person sends a short note: what was wrong, what we did, and whether you need to do anything. Read an example of the tone. The example is not a real customer.

A rollback, prepared first

All plans

A rollback is an undo back to the last good version of the store. It is prepared before a live update so there is a way back.

Skimmer and backdoor check

Standard and Priority

After a fix, we look for hidden card-stealing code (a skimmer) and for an unwanted way in (a backdoor). Results are reviewed by the team. This check is not on Essentials.

A person reviews the risky changes

How the plans differ

On Essentials and Standard, routine updates go through standard checks, and the team still monitors. If a change is large, or it touches payment, a person reviews it before it goes live. On Priority, a person reviews every update. Before you start, we will say in writing which changes count as large.

How a store is taken on

  1. 01

    You write

    A person replies and asks what the store runs on. Early access does not commit you.

  2. 02

    Health check

    We look at the site with you and agree access. Passwords are never requested by email.

  3. 03

    Copy and checkout test

    We set those up before the first live change. Host-level PHP updates are requested from the host, in words you can forward.

  4. 04

    Watch, test, update, write

    That cycle is the service. You can cancel any month.

Who it is for

Small businesses running an online store, or another site that holds customer data, on WooCommerce, WordPress, Magento, or Shopware. All of those sit on PHP, which is the software involved in the October 2026 PHP2Shell holes. What that week means for a shop.

Not sure what your store is built on? Ask anyway. “Not sure” is a complete answer on the form.

Who it is not for

  • A company that already has a security team and a patch process it trusts.
  • A platform we do not work on. We will say so rather than pretend.
  • Anyone who wants a guarantee that a break-in is impossible.

What we are honest about

Scanners mostly report problems. Hands-on patching for larger Magento shops already exists, often at a much higher monthly price. CyberWall is meant to sit between those, for a small PHP store, with a person in the loop. It is an extra careful step. It is not a new category of magic, and it does not replace your host.

The price ranges behind that comparison are a rough estimate from the site owner, not quotes. They are written out on the pricing page.

What we need from you

Enough access to make a test copy and apply updates. We agree that up front, ask only for what the work needs, and explain why. We never ask you to email a password.

The private copy is meant to stay in a private, access-controlled place and to be removed when you leave. The exact location and retention are not published yet. They will be confirmed in writing before any customer is taken on. Privacy and terms are not published yet.
