Services

What our people do for your store

Everything below is done by our team, supported by monitoring tools, and explained to you in plain English.

What's included

Fast security updates

Our team watches for new security holes every day, sometimes before the big public lists catch up, and moves quickly when one affects your store.

Safe testing on a copy

Before anything touches your live store, we try the fix on a private copy (what techies call “staging”). If it breaks, only the copy is affected.

Checkout safety checks

We test that customers can still add to cart and pay, because a secure store that can't take orders isn't much use.

Plain-English updates

After each fix, a real person on our team sends a short note: what was wrong, what we did, and whether you need to do anything.

Skimmer and backdoor check

After fixes, we look for hidden card-stealing code (“skimmers”) and unwanted ways in (“backdoors”). Included on Standard and Priority plans.

Rollback safety net

A rollback is a quick “undo” to the last good version of your store. It's prepared before every live update, so there's always a way back.

Human review for risky updates

Some updates are bigger or touch sensitive parts of your store. For those, one of our people reviews the change by hand. On the Priority plan, a real person reviews every update.

Which of these are included depends on your plan. See pricing for the details.

How it works

Four steps, and most of them happen without you lifting a finger.

  1. Connect your site

    We start with a one-time health check and connect our monitoring. We'll guide you through it.

  2. We watch

    Our team keeps an eye on new security holes for the software your store uses.

  3. We test on a copy

    We try the fix on a private copy and test that customers can still add to cart and pay.

  4. We fix and tell you

    We update your live store and send a short note that explains what changed.

Stores we're built for

We focus on small online stores and customer-data sites built on WooCommerce, WordPress, Magento and Shopware. All of these run on PHP, the behind-the-scenes software involved in the recent “PHP2Shell” news below.

Not sure what your store is built on? Don't worry, just ask us. A real person will help you find out.

What we are honest about

No one can promise a store will never be hacked, and we won't either. CyberWall is an extra protective step that didn't exist before: we fix known security holes quickly and safely, and we reduce the risk. We're not a magic shield, and we don't replace good passwords, backups, or your hosting provider.

Why this matters now

A real example from this month: “PHP2Shell”

PHP is the behind-the-scenes software that powers a huge number of online stores, including WooCommerce, WordPress, Magento and Shopware. In October 2026, two serious security holes were found in PHP itself.

Patched on 21 Oct 2026Two flaws (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619), each rated 9.2 out of 10 for severity, got a fix from the PHP team.
Very wide reachThey affect PHP going back to version 5.4, so the software behind many stores, even when a store's own code is perfectly clean.
Not fixed by plugin updatesUpdating your store's plugins won't fix this. The PHP software on the server has to be updated.

Early attack attempts were reported before the fix was public, and researchers have since shown working code execution against test servers. At the same time, security firm Sansec notes that attacks need specific conditions to work, so this isn't a reason to panic. It is a good reason to make sure someone is on top of it.

This is exactly the kind of moment CyberWall exists for. Our team notices news like this early, works out what it means for your type of store, tests the fix on a private copy first, checks that checkout still works, and then helps get it onto your live store, with a plain-English note so you know what happened.

Sources: Sansec Forensics Team, “PHP2Shell: PHP RCE (CVE-2026-109618, CVE-2026-109619)”, 21 Oct 2026 (sansec.io); reporting from SecurityWeek; US CISA vulnerability notices; and the PHP project's own release announcement (PHP Foundation / php.net). A “CVE” is just the public ID number given to a known security hole.
